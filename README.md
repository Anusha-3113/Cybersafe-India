# CyberSafe India

**Cybersecurity awareness and practical guidance for everyday people in India.**

CyberSafe India is a free, privacy-first educational prototype designed to help people recognize online scams, understand what to do next, and find official reporting resources—especially when fear and confusion make it difficult to think clearly.

> This project began with something that happened to my own family.

## The story behind CyberSafe India

One day, my father received a phone call from someone claiming that they had kidnapped his daughter—me. The caller demanded money and then disconnected.

At first, my father did not suspect that it was a scam. He believed I was at college, so the situation felt confusing rather than immediately unbelievable. About an hour later, the fraudsters called again and threatened him, demanding more money. During the call, he could hear a girl crying out in my native language, saying, “Daddy, save me.”

He could not tell whether the voice was really mine. Panic took over. He tried calling me, but I was in class and could not answer. My father did not know whom to contact, what steps to take, or how to check whether the threat was real. The people around him were unsure how to help, too.

It happened close to lunchtime. When I finally called him back during my break, he asked where I was and whether I was safe. Only then did he explain the calls and threats. I was safe—but I kept thinking about what might have happened if I had not called him back when I did.

That experience stayed with me. It showed me that a scam does not only exploit technology; it exploits fear, trust, urgency, and the absence of clear guidance at the moment it is needed most.

My father is not highly educated, and I realized that knowing that scams exist is not the same as knowing what to do when one happens to you. People may have heard general warnings from banks, the Reserve Bank of India, or automated messages before calls. But when someone says a loved one has been kidnapped, pretends to be a bank employee, impersonates an authority, asks for an OTP, or threatens to expose private information, a warning remembered from another day may not be enough.

People need simple, calm, trustworthy instructions they can understand in a stressful moment—not judgment, complicated terminology, or another place that leaves them wondering what to do next.

**That is why I started CyberSafe India: to turn a frightening personal experience into practical digital-safety awareness that ordinary people can access and understand.**

## The motive

The goal is to make cybersecurity guidance more approachable and actionable for people who may not be technically confident, digitally literate, or familiar with official reporting systems.

CyberSafe India aims to help people:

- Recognize common online fraud and cybercrime scenarios.
- Pause and verify suspicious claims instead of acting under pressure.
- Understand immediate safety steps for financial fraud, account compromise, phishing, impersonation, threats, and online harassment.
- Find official Indian reporting channels and relevant emergency resources.
- Learn before a crisis through short, approachable awareness material.
- Access guidance in a more natural way through voice interaction and, as the project develops, multiple Indian languages.

This is an awareness and guidance project, not a promise that every scam can be prevented or every loss recovered. Its purpose is to help people make more informed decisions and reach the appropriate official support sooner.

## What makes CyberSafe India different?

### 1. Voice-first guidance

When people are worried, typing a detailed question or navigating complicated websites can be difficult. The prototype includes voice input where the browser supports it, along with an option to read guidance aloud. People can also type a question if voice input is unavailable.

### 2. Simple, scenario-based explanations

The project focuses on practical situations—such as a suspicious money demand, a hacked account, or online threats—and gives straightforward next steps instead of relying only on technical vocabulary.

### 3. Awareness before an incident

The aim is not only to help after something goes wrong. Short, accessible awareness videos and learning material are part of the project vision so people can recognize warning signs before they are targeted. Video content can be added and expanded as the project grows.

### 4. Multilingual accessibility

Language should not become another barrier to safety. Supporting people in their own languages is a core goal of CyberSafe India. The current prototype is English-first; voice and multilingual capabilities need to be tested and expanded across supported browsers and languages before being described as fully available.

### 5. Official reporting resources

The prototype points users toward official Indian resources, including the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/) and the cyber-fraud helpline **1930**. For immediate danger, contact **112**. These resources are included to help users find the appropriate channel; this independent project is not a government service.

## Current prototype

The current website prototype includes:

- A voice-input interface, where supported by the browser.
- Read-aloud responses using browser speech synthesis, where supported.
- Basic guidance for financial scams, compromised accounts, and online harassment or threats.
- A complaint-draft helper that organizes details for the user to review.
- Links to official Indian reporting and legal-information resources.
- Reminders to preserve evidence and never share OTPs, passwords, UPI PINs, or full financial account details.

The complaint helper creates a draft locally in the browser. **It does not submit a complaint, contact the authorities, or send the draft to a server.** Users should review all details and submit information only through the appropriate official channel.

## Privacy and safety principles

- **Privacy-first:** The prototype does not require users to create an account to use its basic guidance.
- **No false authority:** CyberSafe India is an independent educational project and is not affiliated with the Government of India, the RBI, banks, or law-enforcement agencies.
- **No guarantee of recovery:** Guidance cannot guarantee that money or an account will be recovered.
- **Protect sensitive information:** Do not enter passwords, OTPs, UPI PINs, or full card or bank-account numbers into the tool.
- **Verify official information:** Procedures, laws, contact details, and reporting channels can change. Confirm them through official sources.

## Who is this for?

CyberSafe India is intended for everyday internet users, families, older adults, first-time digital-payment users, and anyone who may need clear guidance when facing a suspicious online interaction. It is particularly motivated by people who are often left out when digital safety information is too technical, too difficult to navigate, or unavailable in a language they understand.

## My vision

I want digital safety to be something people can understand and act on—not something they only hear about after becoming victims.

My father’s experience made this problem personal. CyberSafe India is my attempt to make sure that, in a frightening moment, a person has somewhere to begin: a calm explanation, a few practical steps, and a path toward trustworthy help.

**Because no family should have to face a moment of fear without knowing what to do next.**

## Important resources in India

- [National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal](https://cybercrime.gov.in/)
- **1930** — helpline for reporting suspected financial cyber fraud
- [Emergency Response Support System — 112](https://112.gov.in/)
- [Reserve Bank of India](https://www.rbi.org.in/)

If money has been transferred through a suspected scam, contact your bank or payment provider immediately and report the incident through the official channels. Preserve call details, messages, screenshots, transaction references, and timestamps. Never share an OTP or UPI PIN with someone who contacts you.

## Project status

CyberSafe India is an evolving educational prototype. Voice features depend on browser support, and multilingual guidance and awareness content will require further implementation and testing. Please verify important instructions with official sources.
